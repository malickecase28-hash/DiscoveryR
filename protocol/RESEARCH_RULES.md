# Research rules

1. Scientific unit: the anchor phenomenon/lifecycle is the research unit; a
   timeframe stream is not the research universe.
2. One causal information clock: at anchor time `t`, only information actually
   available by `t` is eligible. Occurrence/origin timestamps remain provenance.
3. Native scale preservation: a shared causal clock does not make 15s, 1m, 4h,
   and tick objects statistically identical; native scale remains attached.
4. Lifecycle-first: future completion/termination cannot define an earlier-state
   cohort. Completed may be a later outcome or later anchor.
5. Exposure classes: E1 PHENOTYPE, E2 SAME_DOMAIN, and E3 CROSS_DOMAIN are
   information-permission classes, not mandatory progression gates.
6. Discovery versus confirmation: exploratory discovery is valid, but is not
   independent confirmation.
7. A method challenge is not confirmation or replication.
8. Preserve evidence states: DISCOVERY, CANDIDATE, CONFIRMED, REJECTED, NULL,
   and INCONCLUSIVE.
9. Retain null, rejected, and inconclusive knowledge with scope and provenance.
10. When normalizing, preserve the raw value, normalized value, and basis.
11. If detector B derives from A, A+B is not automatically independent confluence.
12. Detector architecture/semantics may be exposed when authorized; prior
   behavioral winners must not contaminate blind question/authority work.
13. This program characterizes detector/lifecycle/context behavior. Strategy
   construction belongs to a later separate program.
14. Agents may research, toolsmith, and review within assignment; scientific
   promotion/freeze remains governed by the human research director.
15. Submission lifecycle: agents leave exactly the required artifacts in their
   private workspace; host orchestration validates and publishes them only as
   an explicit, reviewable action on a dedicated branch.
16. Temporary workspace lifecycle: every phase-scoped temporary workspace must
   be deleted after the phase receives signoff. Preserve only published
   artifacts, required manifests/receipts, and reusable scripts moved into
   their permanent repository location. If signoff is absent, the workspace
   remains intact and is marked pending; it must not be silently reused or
   abandoned. Cleanup is a host-controlled, auditable action after validation.

All work preserves chronology, separates raw observations from derived findings,
reports unresolved semantics and missing data, does not infer provider/model/
peer/orchestrator identity, and writes only to the assigned private workspace.
Agents do not receive Git credentials or remote-publication authority.
